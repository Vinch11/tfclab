# Long rides and endurance hours

## Evidence and limits

- Joe Friel, *How to Have a Personal Best Ironman* (2012): individual training is not universal; cycling specificity and a stable training base matter. https://joefrieltraining.com/how-to-have-a-personal-best-ironman/
- Joe Friel, *Ironman training: how to maximise the build phase* (2017, updated 2022): five rides of 5–6 hours during nine build weeks, not 5–6 hours every week; goal race power and nutrition are important. https://www.220triathlon.com/training/long-distance/ironman-training-how-to-maximise-the-build-phase
- Hunter et al. (2025), *Durability as an index of endurance exercise performance*: deterioration depends on intensity, duration, training status, nutrition and conditions; this review does not establish optimal long-ride durations. https://doi.org/10.1113/EP092120
- Bosquet et al. (2007), taper meta-analysis: reduced volume with maintained frequency/intensity; not evidence for long rides during race week. https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e31806010e0

## Operational coaching references

These are conservative coaching ranges, not universal scientifically proven prescriptions or an endorsement by elite coaches. IM base 150–210 min, build 210–300 min, specific peak 270–330 min; 70.3 base 120–150 min, build 150–210 min, peak 180–240 min. Six-hour rides are not universally imposed. Workstreams prioritising threshold/VO2 keep the first half of the range in base/build; endurance/durability workstreams can reach the upper end. Specific peak follows race preparation. Existing declared-level and injury reductions remain, and recovery reduces the reference by 30%. Taper/race retain existing activation rules.

A standalone bike ride is reserved within existing session quotas; a brick's total duration never certifies its bike leg. When recovery leaves no autonomous bike slot, its brick slot is reassigned to cycling, without adding a session or endurance hours. Running-focused maintenance remains short and LCW retains its own stage rules. Unknown phase retains the prior floor rather than guessing a phase. Insufficient calendar or volume remains visible, not waived.

Weekly endurance-hour ceilings exclude strength. Strength remains in session counts, day limits, recovery, total prescribed time and physiological load. A 17.5-hour endurance budget with two 45-minute strength sessions is 19 hours of total scheduled activity, not 17.5 hours of total availability. Recorded historical total load is not retrospectively reclassified as endurance-only.

Existing plan archives are not rewritten. New generations and regenerated weeks use the policy. No real athlete plan or Nolio calendar is written by this change.
