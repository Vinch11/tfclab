## Sources verified

1. Joyner & Coyle, 2008. *Endurance exercise performance: the physiology of champions*. DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.2007.143834. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2375555/ . Review supporting the physiological dimensions VO₂max, threshold and efficiency. Not a normative grid.
2. Assis et al., 2024. *Maximal Oxygen Uptake, Muscular Oxidative Capacity, and Ventilatory Threshold in Amateur Triathletes: Eight-Month Training Follow-Up*. DOI 10.2147/OAJSM.S453875. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10950087/ . Original full-text Table 2 read. N=13, seven men and six women, maximal treadmill cardiorespiratory testing. First assessment: VO₂max 56.3 ± 5.56 ml/kg/min, MAS 16.69 ± 1.65 km/h. Target preparation: seven 70.3 and six Ironman. Mixed small sample; not a validated ambition, sex/age or race-format norm. These are contextual descriptive statistics only, never optimal intervals or percentiles; no cyclist reference or MLSS derived from VT/RCP. Only first assessment used, no temporal progression displayed.
3. Machado et al., 2025. *Unveiling biophysical characteristics in different triathlon race formats: a systematic review*. https://www.aimspress.com/article/doi/10.3934/biophy.2025021?viewType=HTML . Published review abstract checked: 30 included studies, 18 short and 12 middle-distance formats. Contextual synthesis only; no numerical Ironman ambition grid extracted.

## Remaining limitations

This audit establishes usable contextual evidence and identifies unsupported normative claims; it is not an exhaustive systematic review of all sports/distances. No numerical scientific ranges were introduced for missing ambition-specific references. Additional primary cohorts are needed before certifying the full matrix. No automatic targets, plans, laboratory certification or demographic norms change.
